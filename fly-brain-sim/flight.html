The same spiking network as the brain map, now inside a body. The fly's eyes see a lamp in the arena, photoreceptors drive the optic lobes, steering descending neurons bias the wing steering muscles, and the wing motor neurons provide thrust. Nothing tells the fly where to go: it turns toward the light because that is how the circuit is wired.

How the fly flies

Take-off is a giant-fiber burst: it drives the wing and leg motor neurons at once, the classic escape jump. Once airborne the halteres beat, and their afferents keep the flight descending neurons active, which keep the wing motor neurons firing. That loop is what sustains flight. The lamp's brightness on each eye becomes a photoreceptor firing rate; through lamina, medulla and lobula it reaches the LC visual projection neurons and then the DNa02-like steering descending neurons, whose left/right difference sets the yaw rate. Landing interneurons inhibit the flight descending neurons and the fly drops onto whatever is under it.

What to try

  • Click the ground behind the fly. It has to wander until one eye sees the lamp, then it commits to the turn.
  • Turn synaptic strength down to 0.6×: the steering pathway stops propagating and the fly flies straight.
  • Turn it up to 2×: the whole brain saturates and steering becomes erratic.
  • Press Land mid-flight, then watch the autopilot take off again when the wing motor neurons fall silent.
